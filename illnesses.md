# Ten of the most common illnesses worldwide

**Research date:** 28 October 2026. **Geography:** worldwide. **Population:** all ages where available; age restrictions are stated below.

## Main finding

A defensible global list includes **upper respiratory infections, diarrhoeal diseases, headache disorders, tooth decay, anaemia, hypertension, metabolic fatty liver disease, obesity, severe gum disease, and diabetes**. Several affect more than a billion people or cause billions of episodes annually. Asthma and influenza are also very common; their evidence and placement are explained separately.

**This is an evidence-based ten-condition shortlist, not a verified universal rank from first to tenth.** Acute infections are usually counted as episodes per year; persistent conditions are counted as people affected, and recurrent headaches use a one-year prevalence measure. Comparing those numbers as if they measured the same thing would produce a misleading ranking. The selection below combines two exceptionally frequent acute illness categories with eight very widespread persistent or recurrent clinical conditions. It does not establish that these are the ten highest values across every possible definition of illness.

## Research notes on each illness

### 1. Upper respiratory infections, including colds

This category concerns infections of the upper airway and includes several clinical illnesses. It should not be relabelled “12.8 billion common colds”: the source estimates the broader URI category. It also identifies influenza viruses among possible URI pathogens. Children younger than two had the highest incidence rates in the 2021 study. Repeat episodes explain how the annual count exceeds the world's population. [GBD respiratory-infection study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11680489/).

### 2. Diarrhoeal diseases

Diarrhoea generally means at least three loose or liquid stools daily, or more frequent loose stools than normal for the individual. Intestinal infections can be viral, bacterial, or parasitic; contaminated food or water and person-to-person transmission are relevant pathways. Fluid and electrolyte loss can cause dehydration. The category is broader than food poisoning and is unrelated to influenza despite the informal phrase “stomach flu.” WHO separately reports nearly 1.7 billion childhood episodes annually; that is not an all-age total. [WHO diarrhoeal-disease fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/Diarrhoeal-disease).

### 3. Headache disorders

The estimate combines migraine and tension-type headache within the GBD framework. The study uses one-year prevalence and models time spent symptomatic separately. Thus, 2.9 billion affected people does not mean 2.9 billion people have daily headaches. A grouped category can also outnumber a single diagnosis such as permanent-tooth caries; that does not contradict statements identifying caries as the most common individual health condition. [GBD 2023 headache study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41240916/), [WHO oral-health fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/oral-health).

### 4. Tooth decay

Acids produced by dental plaque from sugars progressively damage teeth. Decay may lead to pain, infection, and tooth loss. The 2023 study additionally estimated 509 million people with untreated caries in baby teeth; adding that figure to permanent-tooth caries would not automatically give a unique-person total. [WHO oral-health fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/oral-health), [GBD 2023 oral-health study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13501145/).

### 5. Anaemia

Anaemia involves too little haemoglobin or too few red blood cells to meet the body's oxygen-carrying needs. Iron deficiency is one cause; infections, blood loss, inherited disorders, and chronic disease can also contribute. [WHO anaemia fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/anaemia).

The measurement update matters: applying the older elevation adjustment to 2023 data yielded about 1.93 billion cases, versus 2.13 billion with the newer method. That roughly 198-million difference reflects the adjustment method; it is not evidence of that many newly ill people in one year. The study also identifies smoking adjustment as remaining work. [2026 anaemia study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13160282/).

### 6. Hypertension

Hypertension can persist without noticeable symptoms while increasing the risk of heart disease, stroke, and kidney damage. WHO's cited definition uses blood pressure of at least 140/90 mmHg, confirmed on different days. Estimates using a different diagnostic threshold will differ. The 1.4-billion figure covers ages 30–79 and includes people who have not been diagnosed; WHO estimates that 44% of affected adults are unaware of their condition. [WHO hypertension fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hypertension).

### 7. Metabolic fatty liver disease / MASLD

MASLD is the current terminology used by the cited study for metabolic fatty liver disease, previously studied as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. The GBD estimate covers 204 countries and territories and is based on models rather than a worldwide census of liver scans. The 1.3-billion figure describes people with the condition in 2023, not people with cirrhosis or liver failure. Do not replace its all-population percentage with an adult-only prevalence percentage from a different study. [GBD 2023 MASLD study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41990758/).

### 8. Obesity

WHO recognizes obesity as a chronic disease influenced by biological, environmental, social, and behavioural factors. Its October 2026 update reports 2024 estimates, superseding the frequently repeated 2022 figures of 890 million adults and over one billion people overall. For adults, WHO uses BMI of at least 30 for obesity; children use age-specific criteria. The 2.6 billion overweight adults include adults with obesity and should not be added to the obesity count. [WHO obesity fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/obesity-and-overweight).

### 9. Severe gum disease

Periodontitis damages the tissues supporting teeth and can lead to tooth loss. It is distinct from decay of the tooth itself, although both can occur in one person. The billion-person estimate concerns severe periodontitis, not mild gum inflammation. [WHO oral-health fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/oral-health), [GBD 2023 oral-health study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13501145/).

### 10. Diabetes

Diabetes involves persistently elevated blood glucose and can damage blood vessels, nerves, eyes, and kidneys. The adult estimate comes from 1,108 population-representative studies with 141 million participants, modelled across 200 countries and territories. The research definition included raised fasting glucose, raised HbA1c, or diabetes medication use. It therefore captures more than already diagnosed patients. Differences in age limits, biomarkers, and modelling can explain why other diabetes reports give different totals. [NCD-RisC primary study](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/496803/), [WHO diabetes fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/diabetes).

## Method and limits

- **Question interpreted:** Which clinical illnesses or established illness syndromes affect exceptionally large numbers of people worldwide? The selection prioritizes frequency, not death toll, treatment cost, or severity.
- **Evidence searched:** WHO fact sheets and original global analyses, including GBD disease-specific papers, NCD-RisC population studies, and relevant primary meta-analyses. Commercial market reports and unsourced “top ten” articles were not used as evidence.
- **Selection rule:** Retain two demonstrably high-frequency acute illness categories, then eight persistent/recurrent clinical conditions with verified population estimates at roughly 800 million or more. This is a transparent editorial selection, not an exhaustive extraction and ranking of every disease in a single dataset.
- **Scope decisions:** Headache disorders, anaemia, and diarrhoeal diseases are included as established clinical syndromes/categories, so this is not a list restricted to ten individual pathogens or ten single-cause diagnoses. Isolated sensory impairments, injuries, and the full set of asymptomatic persistent infections were not systematically ranked. Broad parent groups such as “all oral diseases” were not added alongside their component conditions.
- **People versus episodes:** A person can have multiple infections annually and several chronic conditions simultaneously. **Do not add these counts to estimate a total population, and do not divide an annual episode count by population and call it a percentage of people affected.**
- **Prevalence windows:** One-year headache prevalence and population estimates for persistent conditions have different time meanings. They are presented together to communicate scale, not to assert a common point-prevalence measure.
- **Age denominators:** Hypertension covers ages 30–79; diabetes covers adults aged 18+; other estimates have their own disease-specific eligible ages. Adult percentages are not percentages of all humanity. Age-standardized rates are also different from crude population percentages.
- **Uncertainty:** A 95% UI describes uncertainty in modelled estimates; a confidence interval or Bayesian credible interval has a different statistical interpretation. Intervals are reproduced with the source's terminology where available. Rounded counts cannot support fine rank differences.
- **Freshness:** Research was conducted on 28 October 2026, but underlying data range from 2020 to 2024. Publication/update date is not observation year. No value here is presented as a directly observed worldwide 2026 count.
- **Depth and completeness:** This was a targeted research review, not a registered systematic review or a complete GBD database export. Allergic rhinitis, inherited blood traits, sensory disorders, and other broad categories could alter a differently scoped ranking. Current evidence does not justify labelling this an official worldwide top ten.
